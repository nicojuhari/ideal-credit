## What Works

- **2026-09-22, /unique-value-strategy:** the strongest-testing strategy elements
  are the ones already operating quietly, not invented for the exercise - the
  phone-first/remote-analysis intake model and the refinanțare page's retirement
  (decided independently, 2026-09-11, for a lead-quality reason) turned out to be
  the same underlying mechanism the new strategy leads with. When a positioning
  statement passes the Fit test this cleanly, it's a sign the words were the weak
  link, not the business. See `brand/strategy.md`.

## What Doesn't Work

- **2026-09-22, /unique-value-strategy:** the "Local Growth Partner" headline
  statement blended a saturated category claim ("fast" / "rapid" - every OCN in
  the competitive landscape claims this) with an unfalsifiable one ("genuine local
  knowledge" - not observable by a customer without being told). Combining a
  generic claim with a vague one in the same sentence weakens both; don't lead
  copy with "rapid" as a differentiator, and don't rely on adjectives ("genuine,"
  "real," "true") to carry a claim that needs a stated mechanism instead. See the
  stress-test scorecard in `brand/strategy.md` for the full breakdown.
- **2026-09-22, /direct-response-copy:** a mechanism framed by *timing* ("we tell
  you before you apply") is fragile - it invites the obvious question "before
  what, exactly, if you need my info to analyze anything?" A mechanism framed by
  *substance* (what specifically gets analyzed - the goal and the financial
  situation, not just the file - and why - a correct decision) doesn't have that
  problem, because it's not making a claim about sequence at all. When writing a
  differentiation mechanism, prefer "we look at X and Y to decide Z" over "we tell
  you before/after W" - the former survives scrutiny, the latter invites a logic
  check the business may not actually pass.
